PSS is an AI thinking tool for questions with more than one good answer.
It develops an idea, explores other directions, and lets you compare and keep the useful parts.

Use it when you’re stuck on the obvious answer—not when you just need to look up a fact.

How it works in 20 seconds
  1. Bring a question. Give it a goal, a constraint, or something you’re uncertain about.
  2. Explore the differences. PSS lets the model commit to an approach, then introduces checkpoints where it can branch into alternatives. Different paths aren’t guaranteed to be equally good.
  3. Make it yours. Compare assumptions and tradeoffs, keep useful thoughts, add your own notes, and leave with a next move.

It generates possibilities, not verified research. Check factual claims before acting on them.

Good questions to try:

What makes a good question? What does exploration size mean?

Ask an open-ended “How could…?”, “What if…?”, or “What are different ways to…?” question. Include what you want and one or two real constraints. PSS is less useful for simple facts, arithmetic, or questions with one correct answer.

Focused allows up to 3 thoughts; Wider allows up to 6. That includes the starting thought. These are limits, not a promise of a certain number of answers. More thoughts can take longer on your local model.

How do Paths and Map work?

Paths is a reading list of the directions explored. Map places thoughts around your question and connects each branch to the thought it grew from. Position isn’t a quality ranking. Select any thought to read it; zoom in for detail or Fit to see the whole map.

Check 2 or 3 thoughts and choose Compare to read them side by side. Look for different assumptions, not just different wording.

Where is this saved? Which export should I use?

Notes and kept thoughts save automatically in this browser on this computer. Clearing browser data can remove them; they are not synced to an account.

Download takeaways gives you a readable Markdown document with your selections and notes. Export full workspace gives you a JSON backup you can reopen with Import workspace.
